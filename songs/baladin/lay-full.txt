[Verse 1]
Sing, wine-dark Sea, that rocks the drowned to sleep,
Of Baladin the farseer and his shame:
How he was lost upon the restless deep,
And found again, and how he gained his name.

[Verse 2]
High in the peaks of Pel Regat, where snow
Encircles rock and crowns the Maudrim stair,
Kyme, iron-browed, in ages long ago,
Was crowned, and ruled the Dynan, stern and fair.

[Verse 3]
And Baladin, his son, whose gaze could find
The hawk against the sun at blazing noon;
Yet all he saw could never sate his mind,
And so he sought a darker, older boon:

[Verse 4]
The lore that tore the Veils when time was young,
The words no mortal man was meant to know.
He spoke them in the dark with reckless tongue;
The Veils writhed, and each mountain felt the blow.

[Verse 5]
Then, sore afraid, the Dynan elders rose
And sought the cause for which the mountains shook.
They found the prince, bowed weeping in the snows,
And in his hands lay Rel Abyr's own book.

[Verse 6]
In wrath they tore the crown from Kyme's grey head,
And plucked his eyes as ravens pluck the flesh.
In chains, as beasts are bound, their king was led,
That each new dawn should wake his shame afresh.

[Verse 7]
But Baladin they cast into the sea,
And laid on him a curse more cold than death:
To lose his past, so ran their dread decree,
His name, his kin, until his dying breath.

[Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
The man who has no name can have no home.
Go, brothers, go, find him who cannot see.
Restore his name lest he forever roam.

[Verse 8]
The grey surf cast him up on Ire's white sand
With salt upon his lips and in his eyes;
He rose, unnamed, a stranger in the land,
While gulls wheeled high above with mocking cries.

[Verse 9]
He hauled fish nets, he kept the shepherd's fold,
He felled the oak, and shaped the potter’s clay;
But in his heart a hunger, never old,
Gnawed like the tide that eats the cliff away.

[Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
The man who has no name can have no home.
Go, brothers, go, find him who cannot see.
Restore his name lest he forever roam.

[Verse 10]
Now Barathrim of gilded tongue was lord
Of Ire, a king grown strong in craft and guile,
Who prized a man as fighters prize a sword,
Who hid his avarice beneath a smile.

[Verse 11]
He marked the man, his arm and mind well wrought,
With eyes no lofty eagle would despise.
"Unbound, this man could be my doom," he thought,
"But bound to me, the blade by which I rise."

[Verse 12]
Then Barathrim cried out, with tear-wet cheek:
"My son, my son, whom I have mourned for years!
I sought you long, from shore to lofty peak,
And now the gods have dried a father's tears.

[Verse 13]
"See in our eyes the selfsame hawk-keen light,
The selfsame iron sinew in our thighs.
We are the same." And he of farthest sight
Saw not the hook beneath the gilded lies.

[Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
The man who has no name can have no home.
Go, brothers, go, find him who cannot see.
Restore his name lest he forever roam.

[Verse 14]
A man without a true name of his own
Will take the first a stranger offers him;
So Baladin knelt down before the throne;
He took a name of blood: Kaftoralim.

[Verse 15]
He led the hosts of Ire and won its wars;
And took to wife the king's beloved ward;
But Barathrim, unsated, eyed new shores,
And, smiling, fed the nations to his horde.

[Verse 16]
Where Barathrim but looked, his son would go,
To Paltyne, Farport, Gathsrier, and Nane;
And where he went, he laid the cities low,
Till ash and smoke lay heavy on the plain.

[Verse 17]
The widows, bowed amid the mire and blood,
Would name Kaftoralim to babes in dread,
As one might name the plague, or winter flood,
And curse the name while burying their dead.

[Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
The man who has no name can have no home.
Go, brothers, go, find him who cannot see.
Restore his name lest he forever roam.

[Verse 18]
At last his hosts went up the Maudrim stair
To Pel Regat's high peaks of ice and stone;
A song, half-heard, came drifting on the air,
The mountains muttering in words once known.

[Verse 19]
The Dynan host came down to bar his way
And met him spear to spear upon the height;
They neither gave nor asked for grace that day,
Nor knew their banished prince by voice or sight.

[Verse 20]
But there, still chained and bent beneath the yoke,
A blind old man, who toiled as oxen do,
Turned up his face to hear the man who spoke.
That voice was life, was hope, a voice he knew.

[Verse 21]
And Baladin, who saw the farthest, saw
At last what was so near: within that face,
The iron brow, the stern, unbending jaw
Of Kyme, his king, his father and his race.

[Verse 22]
Then, as the ice that binds a river breaks
With thunder at the coming of the spring,
His blood o'ercame the curse; as one who wakes,
He knew his name, his crime, his home, his king.

[Verse 23]
And blinded Kyme, his father, wept for joy,
And, crying "Baladin!" with breaking voice,
Gave back the name he'd given to the boy,
And bade the snows of Pel Regat rejoice.

[Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
The man who has no name can have no home.
Go, brothers, go, find him who cannot see.
Restore his name lest he forever roam.

[Verse 24]
Then all who'd loved old Kyme now bent the knee;
The elders scattered like the autumn leaf;
And with two hosts he crossed the wine-dark sea
To Ire and Barathrim, the false-tongued thief.

[Verse 25]
His borrowed name flung wide the gates of Ire,
And through the cheering streets into the hall
He strode, and found the king in rich attire:
Grown grey and brittle, cunning still, but small.

[Verse 26]
Then Barathrim rose up and spread his hands,
And wept, as once before, with eyes not dim:
"Come share, my son, my crown and all my lands;
You are my child, my own Kaftoralim!"

[Verse 27]
But Baladin, unbending as the peak,
Like granite carved without a fault within,
Gave answer, as the son of Kyme should speak:
"I am not yours. My name is Baladin."

[Verse 28]
Then came his wife, King Barathrim's dear ward,
And stepped between them, braving all his ire;
With trembling hand she stayed her husband's sword,
And with her tears she quenched the warlord’s fire.

[Verse 29]
And he who set the very world ablaze
Let fall his sword to strike the marbled floor.
It rang like bells that tell the end of days.
It sang that Barathrim would take no more.

[Verse 30]
"For you I spent my strength in countless wars,
My youth, my years, the blood of half the earth.
I spare your life, as my belov’d implores;
But crown and lands I take by right of worth."

[Verse 31]
So Barathrim, uncrowned, lived out his years
Within a high and cold and empty room,
With few to love him, none to dry his tears,
To fade, still gilded, into self-wrought gloom.

[Verse 32]
And Baladin took up the crown, and reigned
From Pel Regat's high snows to Ire's white shore;
His father sat beside him, crowned, unchained;
And with his queen he mended what he tore.

[Final Chorus]
Lo, brothers, lo, across the wine-dark sea:
What once was lost has now been found and won.
Ho, brothers, ho; for Baladin can see,
And sees himself at last: his father's son.

Ho, brothers, ho; for Baladin can see,
And sees himself at last: his father's son.
