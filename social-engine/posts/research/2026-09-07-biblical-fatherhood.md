## Summary

The Reformed / confessional world has spent 2025–26 rebuilding a full-throated
doctrine of the father as **head of the household** — the *paterfamilias* — after
decades of treating fatherhood as optional coaching. The argument has three
legs: it's **creational** (natural law, not culture), it's **federal** (the father
answers to God for the state of his house), and it's **discipleship** (if the
father doesn't form his children, the world will). This is dead-center HMS Lead
territory and the account should be planting a flag in it weekly, not
occasionally.

## What's being said

- **Headship is ontological, not a role to opt into.** "Fathers are not called to
  be the head of their families; they just are" (Steve Heitland, *Christ Over
  All*). The passive father hasn't escaped headship — he's just failing at it.
- **Fatherhood is creational / natural law.** Andrew T. Walker and others frame it
  as built into the created order, so the culture can't legislate it away.
- **Discipleship is the whole assignment.** "If you do not disciple your children
  in sexuality… the world will" (John-Michael Bout). Ephesians 6:4 — "bring them
  up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord" — is the recurring text.
- **The goal is not impressive kids, it's kids pointed at Christ.** Several writers
  push back on performative Christian parenting.
- **Where the mainstream take diverges:** evangelical-broad writing still frames
  dad as "co-parent / spiritual encourager"; the Reformed take restores
  *authority* and *final responsibility* language, tied to household economics and
  patriarchy.
- **Live controversy:** Doug Wilson's "household voting" proposal drew national
  press (Christianity Today, July 2026) — household-as-unit thinking is in the
  water and contested. Amplify the fatherhood substance; steer around the voting
  fight unless the owner wants that pillar (that's Make War / politics, not Lead).
